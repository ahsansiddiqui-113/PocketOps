A rent increase feels like a pay cut. The good news: you can usually absorb a 5–10% hike by adjusting a few levers instead of rebuilding your whole budget.

## Step 1: Measure the real impact

Rent is the biggest slice of your **needs**. Before panicking, calculate how many percentage points of take-home pay the increase eats. Use the calculator below to see your new needs percentage.

## Step 2: Check the 55% line

If needs plus debt payments climb above about 55% of take-home pay, you have very little room to flex. That is the signal to act now rather than later.

## Step 3: Trim in this order

1. Recurring subscriptions you forgot about
2. Insurance and phone plans that can be renegotiated
3. Food spending, planned rather than cut cold turkey
4. Only then, wants that matter to you

## Step 4: Negotiate before you accept

Offer a longer lease in exchange for a smaller increase, and ask about waived fees. Landlords often value stability over a few extra dollars a month.
